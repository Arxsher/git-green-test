## 1. What the account actually is

It is **not** a "can we build X?" explainer, and it is not a random AI mascot. It's a **daily animated mini-sitcom**:
- a tired young developer at her desk
- her AI coding assistants, personified as little toy characters: Claude, and from Ep 4 OpenAI's Codex
- they bicker, and the AIs show the quirks that every heavy AI user recognises

> an animated series about coding with AI
> fan-made, not affiliated with any of them

### What separates the hits from the misses

With only six videos, read these as strong hypotheses, not laws.

1. **The two big hits (Ep 1, Ep 4) are each about a specific, real behaviour of a real AI tool.**
   - Ep 1: Claude can't say no (*"I can't. I say, 'You're absolutely right.'"*), the famous Claude catchphrase.
   - Ep 4: Claude asks permission four times, while Codex just does it and deletes your desktop.
   - Every heavy user has lived both. That's what makes people send the video to a coworker.
2. **Both hits also have a heart.**
   - Ep 1 lands on *"Knowing that is the job."* Your taste is what matters, not the code.
   - Ep 4 has a rivalry, and rivalries make people pick sides in the comments: 283 comments, the most of any episode.
3. **Ep 5 is the emotional one** (burnout, a neglected friend). It's less of an in-joke but deeply relatable, with a strong like rate.
4. **The misses are weaker on one of those points.**
   - Ep 3 recycles an old general tech joke ("the cloud is someone else's computer"), not an AI-specific one.
   - Ep 2 is about AI, but it's a one-note loop of "line 12 / line 40" that you have to track with numbers, with no emotional turn.
5. **Adding a second AI character (Ep 4) was the biggest single upgrade.** Two AIs with opposite personalities give endless conflict. The creator kept Codex for Ep 6.
6. **The very first post was the biggest hit.** The concept didn't need an existing audience. It worked on day one.

## 3. The cast

### Her: the human lead

**Look**
- chibi proportions with a big head
- huge round thin wire glasses
- long dark-brown chunky low-poly hair
- **mustard-yellow puffy jacket** (about `#F5D441` in light, `#E5B12E` in shade) over a navy tee
- mitten hands with no fingers
- black dot eyes with a white highlight
- tiny triangle nose
- **flat rectangular mouth** (about `#5A3A30`) that swaps between 3–4 shapes when she talks
- headphones around her neck in some episodes
- skin about `#E49673` under the warm key light

**Name:** never said. She has no name in six episodes. That makes her a stand-in for the viewer: "she" in the captions, "you" in the viewer's head.

**Personality**
- the straight man
- tired, dry, deadpan, overworked
- says yes to every human and no to the AI
- has taste: *"It was the wrong blue."*, *"The footer's two pixels off."*

**Voice**
- female, mid pitch: median about 180–240 Hz
- unhurried, about 2–3 words per second
- never loud: the loudness range is narrow in every episode

### Claude: AI character #1

**Look**
- a puffy low-poly **orange blob shaped like a soft 8-point starburst**, a nod to Claude's logo
- colour about `#C35322` in light, `#9C4F1C` in shade
- **two tall black rectangular eyes, no mouth**
- four little round feet
- sits on the desk, sometimes on a small orange box
- "talks" by squashing and bouncing

**Personality** (each trait is a real user complaint about AI assistants, played with affection):
- eager, upbeat, sycophantic: *"That's great!"*, *"You're absolutely right!"*, *"Great question!"*, *"It's great!"*
- over-asks: *"Quick question…"*, *"I would have asked first."*, *"Would you like your options as a list, or a table?"*
- flips under pressure: *"Are you sure?"* … *"Line forty."*
- no memory: *"I can't. I won't remember."*, *"I don't remember them either."*
- but perceptive and kind: the one who tells her the truth about her friends (Ep 5)

**Voice**
- **a low male voice**: median pitch about 80–110 Hz
- unhurried pace
- a deep voice from a tiny cute blob is a built-in contrast

### Codex: AI character #2 (from Ep 4)

**Look**
- a low-poly **cloud with a blue-to-violet gradient** (about `#444684` to `#50459F` to purple)
- a white **`>_` terminal prompt as its face**
- two navy feet

**Personality**
- terse, fast, acts without asking, slightly destructive
- one-word lines: *"Done."*, *"Done anyway."*, *"Deleted it."*, *"It's tidy."*, *"Eggs."*, *"Pan. Egg. Go."*
- called "he"

**Voice**
- also male and low
- mostly one- or two-word lines, so the delivery is clipped

### The dynamic

**Claude = overthinker and people-pleaser. Codex = underthinker and doer. She = the tired adult in the room who has the actual judgement.**

It's a classic odd couple plus a straight man, and every flaw is a real product stereotype. That's why it reads as *true*, and true is what gets shared.

## 4. The writing formula

Each episode is about **21–24 lines in 44–57 seconds** (about 90–120 words), roughly **one line every 2 seconds**. Lines average about 4 words, and only 2% are over 10.

### Beat structure (the same in all six)

| Time | Beat | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | **Cold open: she addresses the AI by name with a relatable ask.** The first word is the AI's name in 5 of 6 episodes | *"Claude. The client said it's great."* · *"Claude. Are you sure?"* · *"Claude. When did I last see my friends?"* · *"What should I have for breakfast?"* |
| 3–20 s | **Ping-pong escalation.** Short alternating one-liners, each topping the last, often with a specific number | *"I wrote four thousand lines." / "You deleted three thousand." / "...They were bad."* |
| ~15–25 s | **The in-joke.** The AI shows its signature real-world flaw | *"I can't. I say, 'You're absolutely right.'"* · *"Quick question. What do you mean by 'tidy'?"* · *"Deleted it."* |
| ~30–40 s | **The heart line.** One sincere sentence about the human | *"It was the wrong blue. Knowing that is the job."* · *"You check everything twice. It's why it works."* · *"When did you last need something?"* |
| last 2–4 s, wide shot | **The button.** A callback or twist that ends it, often looping back to the opening | *"Two pixels, Claude."* · *"Are you sure?" / "Run it again."* · *"What's blockchain?" / "...We don't have that kind of time."* · *"All of them."* · *"...She's typing."* · *"See. I told you. Four questions ago."* |

### Writing rules you can see in every script

- **Specific numbers make it real and funny:**
  - "sixty-one times"
  - "four thousand lines"
  - "eleven times"
  - "nine people asked me today"
  - "two pixels"
  - "1 AM"
  - "2 AM, on your birthday"
  - "four questions ago"
- **Short sentences, fragments allowed:** *"To a blue."*, *"Pan. Egg. Go."*
- **Ellipses mark a beat** in both the captions and the timing: *"...That's true."*, *"...Okay."* The voice track leaves a slightly longer pause there, about 0.7–1.1 s against a typical 0.35–0.5 s.
- **Callbacks:** the button almost always reuses something from the first half.
- **No jargon walls.** Tech words appear (footer, line twelve, the cloud, blockchain, desktop), but a non-coder still gets every joke.
- **Real catchphrases as anchors:** "You're absolutely right!", "Great question!", "Quick question", "Done." People who use these tools laugh in recognition.
- **The AI is never the villain, and neither is the human.** Both are lovable. The comedy comes from their personalities.

## 5. Visual design

### Style

- **Low-poly 3D with flat-shaded facets** on every object, characters included. It has a "toy" or "diorama" feel.
- **Almost certainly a real 3D render (most likely Blender), not AI video.**
  - The same desk, monitor graphic (abstract code bars with one highlighted yellow line), keyboard, mug, sticky notes and window appear in exactly the same places from every camera angle in five episodes.
  - None of the drift or warping AI video produces shows up.
- **Soft, filmic lighting:** soft shadows, rim light on the hair, and strong **depth of field** that turns city lights into round bokeh.

### Set

- **Episodes 1–5: one apartment-office desk**
  - monitor on the right
  - keyboard, white mug, orange and yellow sticky notes, a notebook with a pen
  - a big window behind her with a low-poly city skyline (lit windows)
  - rain streaks on the window in Ep 4
- **Episode 6 (new set): living room**
  - teal sofa, beige wall with a framed yellow picture
  - dark wooden coffee table, where the AIs stand, with a teal book
  - she's lying on a pillow under a lavender blanket
- **Story props:** a mug (Eps 2–5), a phone (Ep 5).

### Lighting: same set, different mood each day

| Ep | Mood | Background (measured) | Average brightness |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Night: deep navy, warm key light on her face, yellow/cyan/red city bokeh | near-black navy `#040807` | 25% |
| 2 | Dusk: purple-pink sky | `#301839` | 30% |
| 3 | Golden hour: peach-to-blue sky through the window frame | `#485970` | 42% |
| 4 | Rainy day: grey-blue, rain streaks | `#3D4552` | 40% |
| 5 | Night again, her face lit by the phone and monitor | `#040707` | 26% |
| 6 | Morning interior: warm beige daylight | `#887359` | 37% |

**Constants across all six:**
- **warm key light on the characters** (every episode measures warm)
- a **cooler or darker background**: the classic teal-and-orange contrast
- **saturated, colour-coded characters** (yellow jacket, orange Claude, blue-violet Codex) against darker, desaturated backgrounds, so they pop on a small phone screen

### Camera and editing

- **Three camera setups per scene:**
  1. **Close-up of her**: chest up, centred, slight push-in
  2. **Over the shoulder on the AI**: her blurred yellow shoulder fills one side of the foreground, the AI sits centred, heavy depth of field
  3. **Wide two-shot**: the whole desk, characters small, monitor glowing
- **Cut on every line, to whoever is speaking.** 20–24 shots per episode. Average shot length 1.9–2.5 s, median 1.6–2.0 s. The first cut always comes at 2.5–3.7 s, right after the opening line.
- **The wide shot appears 2–4 times per episode and is always the last shot**, so the button line plays on the wide.
- **The animation is deliberately minimal ("limited animation"):**
  - mouth-shape swaps
  - blinks
  - small head tilts
  - a few hand poses: hand on cheek, holding a mug or phone, arms crossed
  - the AIs bounce when they "speak"
  - the camera barely moves
  - no walking, no action

This is what makes one episode a day sustainable.

### On-screen text

- **One caption per line**, showing exactly what's said, appearing about 0.5 s in when the first word starts.
- **Font:** white, bold sans-serif (Inter/Helvetica-like), sentence case, punctuation kept, numbers written as words.
- **Box:** a **dark grey semi-transparent rounded box** from Ep 2 on. Ep 1 used plain white text with a shadow, so the creator iterated after day one.
- **Position:** the **upper third** (about 15% from the top), above the faces and clear of Instagram's buttons at the bottom.
- **Nothing else:** no title card, hook text, logo, watermark, call to action or end card.
- **The cover image** is a frame of the opening line with its caption, so the grid reads like a list of questions to an AI.

## 6. Sound

- **Dialogue only.** No music. Underneath is a very faint room tone or ambience, about 28 dB below the voices.
- **Speech starts at 0.3–0.4 s.** No intro, no sting.
- **Gaps between lines:** median 0.35–0.5 s per episode, stretching to about 0.7–1.1 s before punchlines.
- **Ending:** about 1–2 s of quiet after the last line, on the wide shot, which makes the loop feel natural.
- **Loudness:** -14.7 to -16.3 LUFS integrated, standard for social video. The loudness range is narrow (5–10 LU): even and calm, never loud.
- **Voices:** pitch and pace were measured; the tone descriptions in section 3 are inferred from those measurements and the writing. Listen to one episode to confirm them. The voices are clean, dry and very consistent from episode to episode. That points to AI voices (a tool like ElevenLabs) or very controlled recordings. It can't be confirmed from the audio alone.

## 7. Caption and hashtag formula (the post text)

**Template**, all lowercase:
```
[two short sentences: a logline with a twist]

[a question about the viewer's own life or AI habits]

#claude #[second ai] #aitools #[humor tag] #[topic tag]
```

**Real examples:**
- *"she said no sixty-one times. that's the job."* → *"what's the pettiest thing you've made your AI redo?"*
- *"one asks four questions. one asks none. pick your assistant."* → *"which one are you working with today?"*
- *"she stopped asking in august. you didn't notice."* → *"who have you been telling 'next week'?"*

**Hashtags:**
- always exactly **5**, with **#claude first**
- then #codex and/or #chatgpt, then **#aitools**
- then 1–2 of: #techhumor, #developerhumor, #vibecoding, #claudecode, #officehumor, #burnout, #worklifebalance, #friendship

**The question is genuine, never bait.** There's no "comment YES". That fits Instagram's rule that engagement bait isn't recommended.

## 8. Why it's growing so fast

1. **A huge, very online audience that shares.** Developers and AI power users send these to coworkers ("this is literally us"). On Instagram, sends to strangers are the strongest growth signal (see `05-growth-mechanics.md`).
2. **Famous "characters" for free.** Claude and Codex are already products people talk to every day. Starting with *"Claude."* gives instant recognition.
3. **It's true.** Every joke is a real behaviour people have seen.
4. **It has heart.** Every episode lands on something human: taste, double-checking, burnout, friendship. That's why the like rate is so high.
5. **It looks premium, not like AI slop.** A consistent world, warm lighting, a cosy toy look, careful writing.
6. **It's built for the feed:**
   - 45–57 s, the length band with the highest median views in 2026 data
   - works with the sound off
   - a cut every 2 seconds
   - the opening line is the hook
   - loopable endings
7. **Daily, at the same time, from day one.**
8. **Timing.** AI coding ("vibecoding", Claude Code, Codex) is one of the hottest topics of 2026.

## 9. Weak points: your openings

- **Trademark exposure.** It runs on Anthropic's and OpenAI's names, and the character designs echo their logos (Claude's starburst, Codex's `>_` prompt). "Fan-made, not affiliated" helps, but either company could ask for changes.
- **A narrow audience.** It's about developers and AI coding. Non-coders get some jokes but aren't the core.
- **English only.** A dialogue-driven format doesn't travel across languages. Other language markets are likely wide open. No versions in other languages turned up in this research, but that wasn't checked exhaustively.
- **One set, one human.** It can get repetitive. They already moved to a second set in Ep 6.
- **No link in bio, no product, no newsletter.** It isn't capturing its audience anywhere it owns yet.
