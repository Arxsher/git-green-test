# Your playbook: launching in this format before the crowd

Read `01-account-teardown.md` first. This turns it into a plan you can run with Google AI Pro, ChatGPT Pro and a voice tool (see `04-production-stack.md`).

---

## 0. The one strategic decision: don't be the copy

- **The trap.** If you copy @canwebuildthis one-to-one (a girl with glasses, a Claude blob, a Codex cloud, English coding jokes), you are the second account in a lane that one creator already owns. Their fans *will* comment "this is a copy."
- **Copycats are coming anyway.** Research on 2025–2026 AI formats shows copycats arrive within weeks (`05-growth-mechanics.md`), and the original keeps the crown.
- **What you can freely take** is the **engine**:
  - the format (a human plus personified AIs bickering)
  - the beat structure
  - the pacing
  - the family of look
  - the posting system
- **What belongs to them:**
  - their specific characters and designs
  - their scripts
  - their jokes

Pick a lane where **you are first**:

| Lane | Example | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Your language** ⭐ | The same idea in German, French, Arabic, Spanish, Turkish, Hindi, Portuguese… | The format is pure dialogue, so an English account can't serve these audiences. Very likely nobody owns "the AI sitcom" in your language yet; search Instagram and TikTok in your language to confirm. And you know the local jokes |
| **B. A bigger job** | Office worker, student, marketer, designer, small business owner, freelancer | The original only speaks to coders. Far more people use ChatGPT or Gemini for emails, homework, Excel and presentations |
| **C. A different AI cast** | ChatGPT + Gemini, or ChatGPT + Gemini + Grok | These are the AIs normal people use. Each has a well-known "personality" |
| **D. Combine** ⭐ | e.g. *a tired office worker and her AIs, in your language* | First in your language **and** a much bigger audience |

**Recommendation: lane D.** Same engine, your own world.

## 1. Design your cast

### The human lead

- **One silhouette, one signature colour item, one face accessory.** The original uses big round glasses and a mustard-yellow jacket. Pick your own, for example:
  - a red beanie
  - a green hoodie
  - a bob haircut with a pencil behind the ear
- **Choose a job your audience has.** Their desk, their props and their problems become the jokes.
- **Name.** The original never names her, so she is a stand-in for the viewer. Copy that, or give a short name if your language makes "she/he" awkward.
- **Voice.** Dry, calm, understated. The human is the straight man and never shouts.

### The AI characters (2 is the magic number)

- **Simple chunky shapes in one strong colour each, with a face that's an icon and no mouth.**
  - Lip sync isn't needed: they "talk" by bouncing.
  - Design your own shapes. Don't copy their starburst or `>_` cloud, and don't copy brand logos exactly.
- **Opposite personalities.** This produced the original's biggest jump (Ep 4: 366K views, most comments):
  - one **overthinker**: eager, polite, asks questions, makes lists
  - one **doer**: terse, fast, does it without asking, slightly destructive
- **Voice contrast.**
  - The original gives the cute little Claude blob a **low male voice**, and that contrast is funny on its own.
  - Give each AI a voice nobody would expect from its shape.

### AI personality cheat sheet

Use these affectionately. They're comedy stereotypes that users recognise, not facts:

| AI | Comedy traits people recognise |
|---|---|
| ChatGPT | "Certainly!", very enthusiastic, long answers in bullet points, offers more ("Would you like me to also…?"), remembers things you forgot you told it |
| Gemini | Brings everything back to Google, wants to search first, very cautious, suggests Google Docs and Sheets for everything |
| Grok | Edgy, sarcastic, jokes at the wrong moment |
| Copilot | Finishes your sentences (wrongly), autocompletes your life |
| Siri / Alexa | Mishears everything, "Here's what I found on the web" |
| Perplexity | Cites three sources for "good morning" |
| Claude | Polite, "You're absolutely right!", asks clarifying questions. *The original owns this one; avoid making yours a copy.* |

### Character bible

Fill in one per character and paste it word for word into every image and video prompt:

```
NAME:
ROLE: (straight man / overthinker / doer)
SHAPE + SIZE:
COLOURS (hex):
FACE: (eyes, mouth or no mouth)
SIGNATURE PROP / CLOTHING:
VOICE: (gender, pitch, energy, accent, speed)
CATCHPHRASES (2–3):
NEVER DOES:
```

## 2. The episode formula

**Target:** 45–55 s, 20–24 lines, about 90–120 words, one line every ~2 s.

| Time | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | **Cold open** | The human says the AI's name plus a relatable question or problem. This line is also your cover image |
| 3–20 s | **Ping-pong** | Short alternating lines, each topping the last. Put a specific number in at least one |
| 15–25 s | **The in-joke** | The AI does its signature real-world flaw |
| 30–40 s | **Heart line** | One sincere sentence about the human: their taste, their tiredness, their worth |
| last 3 s | **Button** | Wide shot. A callback to something from the first half. End on a beat of silence so it loops |

**About 1 episode in 4 should be mostly emotional**, like the original's Ep 5 (burnout, friends). It's comedy with a heart.

### Writing rules

1. **Max about 10 words per line.** Average about 4.
2. **Specific beats vague:** "eleven times", "2 AM", "four questions ago", "two pixels".
3. **Use "..." in captions** for a beat before a punchline, and leave a ~0.8 s pause in the audio there.
4. **Every AI line should be something the real AI would plausibly say.** Recognition is the joke.
5. **Nobody is the villain.** The AIs are lovable, and so is the human.
6. **No jargon walls.** Someone's mum should get it.
7. **Punch up by hand.** Read it aloud, cut 20% of the words, check the button calls back.

### Let Claude or ChatGPT draft, then you fix it

Paste this into Claude or ChatGPT, then edit the results by hand. Granny Spills, a 2M-follower AI account, works exactly this way: AI drafts, humans punch up.

```
You write a daily animated mini-sitcom for Instagram Reels.
Cast: [HUMAN: name/job/traits], [AI 1: overthinker, traits, catchphrases], [AI 2: doer, traits, catchphrases].
Language: [LANGUAGE]. Audience: [WHO].
Format: 45–55 seconds, 20–24 lines of dialogue, max 10 words per line, ~100 words total.
Beats: (1) cold open — human says the AI's name + a relatable ask; (2) fast escalation with at least one specific number; (3) the AI shows a recognisable real-world AI habit; (4) one sincere "heart" line about the human; (5) a button line that calls back to the first half.
Rules: dry understated humour, no villains, no jargon a non-expert wouldn't get, use "..." for comic pauses, every AI line must sound like something that AI would really say.
Give me 10 episode loglines first. Then I'll pick one and you write the full script as a two-column table: SPEAKER | LINE, plus a suggested camera (CLOSE-UP / OVER-SHOULDER / WIDE) for each line, and a post caption (lowercase, two short sentences + one genuine question for viewers, + 5 hashtags).
```

## 3. Idea bank: 30 starters

These are written for **a tired office worker + an overthinker AI + a doer AI**. Adapt them to your language and job. None of them repeat the original's episodes.

**Work**
1. **"Make it pop."** The boss's only feedback. The overthinker asks what "pop" means; the doer adds 40 emojis.
2. **"Summarise this meeting."** The summary is one line: "Nothing was decided." Accurate.
3. **Translate my boss.** The AI translates corporate speak: "Let's circle back" means no.
4. **Make this email sound less angry.** Five rewrites later: "Hi."
5. **The doer sends an email before she checked it.** "Sent." / "To who?" / "Everyone."
6. **Honest feedback on the presentation.** The overthinker sugarcoats; the doer: "Slide 3 is bad."
7. **"How long will this take?"** The AI always says "2 hours." It has been 2 hours since Tuesday.
8. **The spreadsheet formula.** The doer fixes it and deletes a column of data.
9. **Two AIs emailing each other.** Hers and her boss's. Nobody reads anything.
10. **A 3 AM startup idea.** The overthinker: "Great idea!" The doer: "Already built it. Zero users."

**AI quirks**
11. **A confident made-up source.** "Who wrote this book?" / "Me. Just now."
12. **The model update.** Her AI has a new personality, and she misses the old one.
13. **The cautious refusal.** "Kill the process." / "I can't help with violence."
14. **"Explain it like I'm five."** Then like I'm 30. Then "like I'm tired."
15. **Too much memory.** The AI remembers the thing she said in March. In front of her friend.
16. **Too polite.** The overthinker thanks the doer; the doer has already finished three tasks.

**Life**
17. **A birthday gift for a friend.** The overthinker asks twelve questions; the doer orders "a mug."
18. **Vacation plan.** The itinerary has "check emails" on day 2.
19. **The 7-step morning routine.** She's on step 0: still in bed.
20. **Mum's message.** The AI drafts a formal email reply; mum wanted a voice note.
21. **The dating profile bio.** "Passionate about synergy."
22. **The gym plan.** The doer books 6 AM. Every day. Forever.
23. **Paperwork and taxes.** Local bureaucracy jokes; very strong in a non-English lane.
24. **Family dinner.** "So what do you actually do?" / "I tell the AIs what to do." / "So they work for you?" / AIs: "...Sure."

**Heart (about 1 in 4)**
25. **"When did you last take a lunch break?"** The AI checks. It's a long list of no.
26. **Mum called four times.** The AI won't help her reply by text: "Call her."
27. **She thanks the AI more than her team.** The AI suggests she tell them instead.
28. **The year-end review.** The AI lists her stats: "You said 'just one more thing' 312 times."
29. **She's sick.** The AIs hide her tasks. The doer: "Deleted them." (Pause.) "Just for today."
30. **First day back after burnout.** The overthinker made a list. It has one item: "Be nice to yourself."

## 4. Producing it

The full detail is in `04-production-stack.md`. In short:

1. **Once (a weekend):** design the cast and set in **ChatGPT Images 2.5**, then make 4–5 lighting moods of the same set (night, dusk, golden hour, rain, morning). The original changes mood every episode, and that keeps the feed fresh.
2. **Once per mood:** build a library of about 10 reusable motion loops in **Google Flow** (Gemini Omni 1.1):
   - human talking and listening
   - each AI bouncing and idle
   - wide shots
   - That's about 70 credits per mood at 360p, plus free upscales, out of your 1,000 a month.
3. **Per episode (about 1–2 hours once you're practised):**
   1. script
   2. voices in **ElevenLabs**, one line at a time
   3. cut to the speaker on every line in **CapCut** or **Edits**
   4. wide shot at the end
   5. captions
   6. faint room tone
   7. export 1080×1920
4. **Later, if it takes off:** move to real 3D in **Blender**, driven by Claude, for perfect consistency. That's the original's likely route.

**Copy the original's caption look:**
- white bold sans-serif
- a dark semi-transparent rounded box
- the upper third
- one caption per line
- appearing on the first word
- no title, logo or call to action on screen

## 5. Launch plan

### Before day 1

- [ ] Pick the lane (section 0), the cast (section 1) and the account name.
- [ ] **Account name.** A question or a phrase works, like "Can We Build This?". Make it short, in your language, and easy to search.
- [ ] **Bio, 3 lines:**
  1. the premise
  2. "an animated series about …"
  3. "fan-made, not affiliated" if you use real AI names, plus "made with AI"
- [ ] **Profile picture:** a close-up of your lead in your best lighting mood.
- [ ] Switch to a **professional (creator) account**.
- [ ] **Bank 7–10 finished episodes** before posting the first. Fruit Love Island died of burnout; don't let that be you.
- [ ] Turn off the Gemini/Flow visible watermark. Never post another app's watermark.

### Every day

- [ ] **Post one episode at the same time every day.** The original posts at 14:00 UTC. For a European audience, try 17:00–19:00 local; then test.
- [ ] **Post text:**
  - lowercase
  - two short sentences (logline plus twist)
  - one genuine question for viewers
  - 5 hashtags: your AI names, `#aitools`, a humour tag, a topic tag
  - **Never** "comment YES"-style bait; Instagram won't recommend it.
- [ ] **Turn on the post's "AI info" label.** It costs nothing, and undisclosed AI profiles can lose recommendations.
- [ ] **Reply to comments for the first hour.** Mine them for episode ideas.
- [ ] **Cross-post** natively, without TikTok watermarks:
  - to TikTok (Creator Rewards only pays on videos of 60 s or longer, so make a slightly longer cut)
  - to YouTube Shorts and Facebook Reels

### Every week

Check each episode's numbers in Instagram Insights:

- [ ] views after 1 h and 24 h
- [ ] **like rate** (likes ÷ views): above 8% is great, and the original's hits are 10–11%
- [ ] **shares/sends**: the most important signal for reaching strangers
- [ ] **skip rate**: if people leave in the first 3 s, the cold-open line is the problem
- [ ] **comments**: rivalry episodes get the most

Then **double down on whatever gets the most sends.** The original's pattern so far:
- a real AI behaviour plus a heart line won
- generic tech jokes lost
- adding a second AI was the biggest jump

**Levers to pull when growth flattens:**
- a new AI character
- a new set
- a two-part story
- "pick your assistant" rivalry episodes
- a heart episode

## 6. Risks to manage

- **Trademarks.**
  - Using real product names in dialogue is how the original gets instant recognition, and it presents itself as "fan-made, not affiliated."
  - Characters that copy a company's logo, or anything that looks official or sponsored, increases risk.
  - Design your own characters, keep the disclaimer, and never imply endorsement.
  - Before monetising heavily, get proper legal advice. This is not legal advice.
- **Copying risk.** Don't reuse the original's characters, scripts or jokes. It invites backlash and reports, and it wastes your one real advantage: being first in *your* lane.
- **AI disclosure.** Label AI use (bio plus the post toggle).
- **Burnout.** Batch production, keep a buffer of 5+ episodes, and use reusable loops.
- **Monetisation.** Take brand deals from AI tools, productivity apps and education. **Never** memecoins or scammy promotions; Jean Phil lost his audience's trust in a day.
